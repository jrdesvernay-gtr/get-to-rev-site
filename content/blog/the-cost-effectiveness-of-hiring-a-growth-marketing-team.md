Startup growth is hard, and most early teams don't have the headcount to cover it with specialists. A fractional growth team — marketers, creatives and analysts brought in on a scoped engagement instead of full-time — is built for exactly that gap.

## Why not just hire one marketing manager?

One full-time generalist has to cover strategy, paid acquisition, content, landing pages and reporting alone. That's a lot to be excellent at simultaneously, and it's a common way marketing managers burn out or plateau. A fractional team splits that work across people who specialize in each piece, coordinated by one lead, for a cost that's scoped and known up front instead of a full salary and benefits package.

## How the fractional model runs leaner

A fractional engagement typically runs on the same discipline as growth marketing itself: short cycles, weekly check-ins, and real-time reporting, with one lead setting strategy while specialists execute. At Get-To-Rev specifically, that lead role is a fractional GTM engineer who also builds the automation — the outbound system, the campaign tooling, the reporting — so the team isn't just executing by hand.

## What the team actually covers

A fractional growth engagement can run paid acquisition across the major platforms, SEO and content, landing page testing, and full-funnel strategy end to end — the same scope a full in-house department would own, scoped to what your stage actually needs.

## The real cost comparison

Get-To-Rev publishes its plans rather than quoting case by case: [Outbound starts at $2,500 a month](/pricing/), GTM Engineering at $5,500, and GTM + Growth Engineering at $8,500, each on a 6-month term. Compare that to a single full-time hire's salary, benefits and tools, and a fractional engagement is often the lower-cost way to get senior, multi-specialist coverage — without the fixed cost of a full-time department before you know the model works.

## Lower risk, more flexibility

Fractional support scales up or down without a long-term employment contract, and if it doesn't fit, the downside is a short initial term rather than a sunk salary and months of ramp time.
