Startups and scaling companies usually face this choice before they're ready to make it: hire a full-time marketing or GTM person, or bring in fractional capacity first. The right call depends less on preference and more on where your growth motion actually stands.

## The two models

A full-time hire is one person, employed, setting strategy and running outbound, content, SEO and reporting themselves. Whatever they can build and run in a normal work week is what your GTM motion gets.

A fractional GTM engineer is senior build capacity, backed by a small team, on a defined term. Instead of one generalist covering everything, a fractional engagement brings in whoever the backlog actually needs, scoped to a plan and a term instead of a headcount.

## Where each one wins

**A full-time hire wins on:** cultural embedding, long-term ownership of a single system, and being in the room for every decision. Once your model is proven, having that person in-house, full-time, is usually the right next step.

**Fractional wins on:** speed to first results (no recruiting cycle or ramp time), access to senior-level build capacity without a full salary commitment, and the ability to scope a short initial term instead of a long one. It also tends to come with existing build patterns — a multi-agent outbound system, campaign automation, attribution reporting — instead of starting from a blank page.

## The real cost comparison

A full-time GTM or marketing hire costs a salary, benefits, tools and the weeks it takes to recruit and ramp before they produce anything. A fractional engagement is priced and termed up front — see [what a fractional GTM engineer at Get-To-Rev costs](/pricing/) for the current plans — with no recruiting cycle and a short initial commitment if it doesn't work out.

## A practical way to decide

Choose fractional when you need fast results, you're still validating product-market fit, or you want senior-level GTM leadership without a full-time budget. Choose a full-time hire once the model is validated and you want one person owning it long-term, in the room, every day.
