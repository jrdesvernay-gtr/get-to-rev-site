Growth marketing is for startup founders, early marketing hires and traditional marketers who want a structured way to find what actually grows the business, instead of running one channel at a time and hoping. It combines marketing with product, data and engineering, and tests across the whole funnel rather than relying on a single tactic.

## Step 1: Shift your mindset

Growth marketing is experiment-driven, not campaign-driven. That means fast tests over big launches, retention and activation getting the same attention as acquisition, close work with product and engineering instead of marketing working in isolation, and treating a failed test as data, not a loss.

## Step 2: Understand your funnel

Most funnels break down into six stages: awareness, acquisition, activation, retention, revenue and referral. Map where people actually drop off before you pick a channel to fix it — the leak is usually earlier in the funnel than the channel you'd default to testing.

## Step 3: Build your team

You don't need a department. Most teams start with a fractional lead or a small fractional team covering a growth lead, a performance marketer, a data analyst and someone who can write and design. Add specialists as the backlog grows past what that core group can run.

## Step 4: Define metrics and OKRs

Pick the handful of numbers that actually matter: CAC, LTV, retention, activation and conversion rate. Set one OKR per quarter tied to a real bottleneck — for example, improving activation in the first week after signup — instead of a long list of metrics nobody checks.

## Step 5: Choose your channels

Common starting channels are SEO and content, paid search, paid social, email and lifecycle marketing, referral programs, and influencer or partner content. Pick two or three based on where your funnel actually leaks, not based on what's trending.

## Step 6: Set up tools and analytics

A working stack usually needs an analytics tool (GA4, or a product analytics tool like PostHog, Mixpanel or Amplitude), a behavior tool (Hotjar or FullStory), a tag manager, and a CRM to track leads through to revenue. The tools matter less than making sure every channel reports into the same place.

## Step 7: Start testing

Start small: an A/B test on a landing page headline, a new ad creative, a different subject line. Write down what you tried and what happened, every time — that log becomes your growth playbook.

## Step 8: Iterate weekly

Run short one- to two-week sprints: pick the test, launch it, measure it, share the result, decide whether to scale or kill it. Consistency compounds faster than any single big win.
